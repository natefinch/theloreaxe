Understanding when to hold up mana and when to tap out is the fundamental tension of tempo-based strategies. In Pauper, where the power level is compressed, these decisions matter even more.

## The Tempo Spectrum

Every game of Magic exists on a tempo spectrum. On one end, you have the aggressive deck that wants to deploy threats and attack before the opponent can stabilize. On the other end, the control deck that wants to answer everything and win in the late game.

Blue tempo decks in Pauper live in the fascinating middle ground. You're not trying to be the fastest deck at the table — that's Red's job. You're trying to deploy an efficient threat and then protect it while disrupting your opponent's game plan just enough.

## Key Cards and Their Roles

The backbone of Pauper blue tempo is a set of cheap, evasive creatures backed by free or near-free protection:

- **Delver of Secrets** — The quintessential one-drop that demands an answer
- **Spellstutter Sprite** — Tempo and card advantage rolled into one
- **Counterspell** — The gold standard of interaction

## When to Hold, When to Fold

The critical decision in every game comes down to this: do you tap out to advance your board, or hold up mana for interaction?

> The best tempo players don't just count mana — they count the opponent's realistic options and weight them against their own clock.

In general, the earlier the game, the more you should lean toward deploying threats. As the game progresses and your opponent has more mana available, the calculus shifts toward holding up answers.
