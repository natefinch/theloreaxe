Commander isn't about winning — it's about the story the game tells. Every deck is a character, every game an unrepeatable narrative. This is my love letter to the format that brought me back to Magic.

## The Social Contract

What makes Commander special isn't the 100-card singleton construction or the legendary creature at the helm. It's the social contract — the implicit agreement between players that the game should be fun for everyone at the table.

This doesn't mean you can't play powerful cards or try to win. It means you should be intentional about the kind of game you want to create.

## Building Characters, Not Decks

I think of my Commander decks as characters in a story. Each one has a personality, a playstyle, and a narrative arc:

- My **Meren** deck is the patient schemer, slowly building an insurmountable advantage from the graveyard
- My **Krenko** deck is the chaotic improviser, always one good turn away from overwhelming the table
- My **Tatyova** deck is the explorer, drawing cards and playing lands in a meditative loop

## The Unrepeatable Game

No two Commander games are alike. With four players, 100-card decks, and the inherent variance of the format, every game tells a different story. That's what keeps me coming back.
