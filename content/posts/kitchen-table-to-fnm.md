Everyone starts somewhere. For me, it was a borrowed deck of mostly commons on a friend's kitchen table. This is the story of how I went from those humble beginnings to competing at Friday Night Magic.

## The Kitchen Table

My first Magic game was terrible and perfect. I had no idea what I was doing. I attacked with creatures that had summoning sickness, I forgot to untap my lands, and I thought Fog was the best card ever printed.

But I was hooked.

## The Learning Curve

The jump from kitchen table Magic to organized play feels enormous when you're standing on the outside looking in. The rules are stricter, the decks are better, and everyone seems to know things you don't.

Here's what I wish someone had told me:

1. **Everyone was new once** — The regulars at your LGS remember what it was like
2. **Your deck doesn't have to be expensive** — Budget builds can absolutely compete at FNM
3. **Losing is learning** — Every loss teaches you something if you're paying attention
4. **Ask questions** — Magic players love talking about Magic

## First FNM

I'll never forget my first FNM. I went 1-3 with a janky green deck that had way too many lands and not enough ways to win. But that one match win — against another beginner with a red deck — felt like winning the Pro Tour.

## The Community

The thing that kept me coming back wasn't the game itself (though the game is great). It was the people. The regulars who offered to look at my deck and suggest improvements. The judge who patiently explained the stack. The opponent who, after beating me, sat down and walked me through what I could have done differently.

Magic is a community game. Don't be afraid to join it.
